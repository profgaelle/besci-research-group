I am the Behavioural Science Lab Manager at Kingston Business School, Kingston University. I manage the School's specialist research lab and support academic staff, researchers and students in designing and running studies with tools such as eye-tracking, facial expression analysis, EEG, galvanic skin response and virtual reality.

Behavioural science and digital technology are at the heart of my work. Using biosensors, we can capture attention, emotion and decision-making as they unfold, revealing what self-report alone cannot. These behavioural insights help organisations design solutions grounded in real behaviour, while working in the lab gives students practical research skills valued in both academia and industry.

Working alongside Professor Gaëlle Vallée-Tourangeau, Professor of Behavioural Science, I have contributed to the following projects:

- Creative cognition and user innovation: I assisted in a multidisciplinary project aiming to identify and compare the antecedents and correlates of creative cognition and behaviour, in two distinct research areas, Human Experimental Psychology and User Innovation.
- Towards Outstanding Research Reviews (TORR): I was involved in the TORR project, funded by the Wellcome Trust. The research aimed to understand how peer-reviewers use information in grant applications to make their recommendations for funding.
- Create for Mental Health: Using behavioural analytics from our lab, I contributed to a project with the charity Create for Mental Health, aimed at helping the charity maximise the impact of its media productions in promoting youth mental health.
- Kingston Chamber of Commerce: Drawing on eye-tracking analytics, I assisted in a user experience study aimed at helping the Kingston Chamber of Commerce develop an online service that is easier to use, more attractive and more community-focused for local business owners and patrons.
- PromoVeritas: I contributed to collaborative research between PromoVeritas and Kingston Business School exploring how winner testimonials and gamification affect people's motivation to enter promotions.
